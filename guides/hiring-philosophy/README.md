# How to hire fractional talent

The old model of hiring assumed you needed lots of people, all the time. Most companies don't anymore.

Teams are smaller. Every hire is a bigger piece of your culture, and a full-time mistake costs more than it used to. At the same time, the most AI-fluent people we know are doing more in a day than most teams do in a week — and they don't have anywhere to put the extra. Their evenings and weekends can be your edge.

A lot of work will be automated eventually. Today, the right unit is still a person running the tools: they bring the taste, the judgement, and the care to get something to "done done." The agent writes the code; the person makes it good.

So hiring isn't a workaround in a world of AI — it's how you scale the human parts.

## Three ways to work with people on Tickets

**1. An ongoing workstream**

Good for: ops work like HR or month-end finance, lower-touch customer-facing roles, engineering work like eval management or owning the quality of a specific feature.

How it works: one 30-minute intro, then a paid 2–5 day trial project. If roughly 30% of the people you try clear the bar, you're calibrated. Many fewer than that and you're being too picky; many more and you're not screening hard enough.

Once they're in, give them an area to own — not a queue of tickets — and assign work in Slack or Linear. The good fits feel almost easy: relaxed timelines, real work, shared wins, warmth in the DMs.

The best candidates are busy with their own thing and want a meaningful side project. Get clean contracts signed on day one — you'll need tidy IP at your next raise. Pay monthly, every month, no exceptions.

**2. A project bet**

Good for: building a CRM in Attio, getting to MVP, making mobile feel great, shipping a specific thing without distracting your core team.

This is how you, as a founder, stay on the quarter's priorities — distribution, the core of the product — instead of getting pulled into delivery and side quests.

The shape is the same as the ongoing workstream: small intro, paid trial, then the work. Lean toward people who bring skills your team doesn't have. Someone between jobs is completely fine.

**3. Hiring through a paid trial**

A lot of teams — Linear is the well-known one — run a real paid project with a candidate before making them an offer. It costs more up front and saves you from a mis-hire, which is painful for everyone, especially the person you'd have hired.

Run a real, paid 2–5 day project. If the fit feels A+, make the offer. [How they hire at Linear](https://linear.app/now/how-we-hire-at-linear) is a generous template if you want one.

Fractional and project work do turn into full-time hires, often. Some of our favourite hires came in that way. The trick is to be honest up front about what you're hoping for, so the person you're working with can be honest back.
